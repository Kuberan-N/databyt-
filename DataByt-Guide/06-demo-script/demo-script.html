1 · Before the Demo

A great demo is 80% preparation. Five minutes of setup makes you look like you've done this a thousand times.

Setup checklist (2 min before the call)

  • Have a DataByt account open with sample data already loaded (Integrations → Load Sample Data)
  • Be on the Dashboard page, logged in, ready to screen-share
  • Close every other tab and notification — zero distractions
  • Have their name + company noted; if you know their rough DSO pain, have it ready to reference

The demo mindset

Show, don't tell. A CFO doesn't want a feature tour — they want to see their problem solved. Every click should answer "what's in it for me." Talk about their pain; let DataByt show the cure.

The golden ratio: They should talk ~40% of the time even during the demo. Pause after each "wow" moment and ask "does that match how your team works?" Keep it a conversation, not a monologue.

Never demo on a broken or empty account. Always pre-load sample data. An empty dashboard kills the moment. (This is why the Load Sample Data feature exists.)

2 · The 15-Minute Demo Flow

Follow this beat by beat. Each step builds on the last toward the close.

1
Frame it 1 min
Set expectations and tie to their pain.
Say
"I'll show you DataByt the way it'd actually work on your AR. You mentioned [their pain — e.g. 'your team spends hours chasing invoices']. Let me show you exactly how that disappears."
2
The Dashboard 2 min
Open on the Dashboard. Point to the 8 live metrics.
Say
"This is your command center. Total AR, DSO, Collection Effectiveness, overdue percentage — live, always current. No more pulling this together in a spreadsheet for the board. Notice DSO and CEI are colour-coded — green means healthy, red means act now."
3
AR Aging — the problem made visible 3 min
Go to AR Aging. Show the buckets and Top Overdue Customers.
Say
"Here's every overdue invoice, grouped by how late it is. And here are your biggest at-risk customers, ranked. Instead of guessing who to chase, DataByt tells you — biggest impact first."
4
The "wow" — AI sends a real email 3 min
Click Email on an overdue invoice. Pick a level. Let the AI draft it live.
Say
"Watch this. I'll pick this overdue invoice, choose the tone — let's say a firm second reminder — and the AI writes the whole email. Personalised, right tone, payment link included. Your team would've spent 10 minutes on this. It took 3 seconds. And it does this for every overdue invoice, automatically, on a schedule."

5
The Collections Pipeline 2 min
Open Collections → Pipeline tab.
Say
"This is the part finance leaders love. Every customer, and exactly where they are — not contacted, first reminder, firm, final notice, disputed, paid. You always know what's happening, without asking anyone. This is the visibility you've never had."
6
The AI Assistant — ask & act 2 min
Open the chat widget. Type a question live.
Say
"And you can just ask it. Watch — 'who owes me the most?'… there. Real numbers, instantly. You can even tell it to send a reminder or file a dispute, and it does it. It's like having an AR analyst on call 24/7."
7
The close to pilot 2 min
Stop sharing. Look at them. Make the offer.
Say
"That's DataByt. Here's what I'd suggest: let's run this on your real AR, free, for two weeks. I'll set it up with you in 48 hours, you approve every email before it sends, and at the end you'll see exactly what we moved. If it's not obviously useful, we part as friends. Want to try it?"

Then stop talking. After you ask "want to try it?" — silence. Let them answer. The first person to speak after the ask loses. Wait.

3 · The "Wow" Moments

Three moments do the selling. Slow down and let each one land — pause, and ask if it matches their world.

MomentWhy it landsThe line
AI writes the email liveThey see hours of work vanish in seconds"3 seconds vs 10 minutes — for every invoice."
The pipeline viewVisibility they've never had into who's where"You'll always know what's happening, without asking anyone."

Tailor the wow to their pain

  • Pain = too much manual work → linger on the AI email + automation
  • Pain = no visibility → linger on the pipeline + dashboard
  • Pain = board / reporting pressure → show the one-click PDF report + live DSO/CEI
  • Pain = disputes dragging → show the dispute pause/resolve flow

Match the demo to what they told you on discovery. A demo that solves their specific pain converts far better than a full feature tour. Show less, but show the right thing.

4 · Demo Do's & Don'ts

Do

  • Pre-load sample data — never demo on empty
  • Reference their specific pain from the discovery call
  • Pause after each wow and ask "does that match how you work?"
  • Show real actions (email drafting, AI assistant) — motion sells
  • Keep it to ~15 minutes — leave them wanting more
  • End with one clear ask — the free pilot
  • Go silent after the ask

Don't

  • Don't tour every feature — confusion kills deals
  • Don't talk for 15 minutes straight — it's a conversation
  • Don't oversell or overpromise — let the product speak
  • Don't show bugs or half-built areas — stick to the strong flow
  • Don't discuss pricing during the demo — pilot first, price later
  • Don't end without a next step — always book the setup or a follow-up

The #1 demo killer: talking too much. Every extra minute of monologue lowers your odds. Show the cure, pause, ask, close.

5 · Onboarding: 48 Hours to Live

They said yes. Now make the first 48 hours flawless — your onboarding is your product experience. Do it with them, not for them.

1
Create their org & access 10 min
Get them logged in. Walk them through the empty dashboard so they understand it'll fill up.
2
Get their data in 15 min
Best: connect QuickBooks/Xero live on a screen-share. If they can't right now, have them export a CSV and you import it together. Confirm invoices appear in AR Aging.
3
Set the essentials 10 min
Together in Settings:
  • Email signature (their company sign-off)
  • Payment link template (their Stripe/bank/any URL)
  • Collection rules (confirm L1/L2/L3 day thresholds)
4
Tag a few key customers 5 min
Mark 2–3 strategic and any at-risk customers so tone is right from day one.
5
Do a dry run together 10 min
Draft one real dunning email with them watching. Let them feel in control. This builds the trust that lets them later turn on full automation.

Set the approval mode for the pilot: Start with the CFO approving emails before they send. Once they trust the AI's writing (usually within days), switch to full automation. Trust is earned, not assumed.

6 · The First-Week Checklist

The pilot succeeds or stalls in week one. Stay close. Drive to a visible win and the Day-14 wrap-up.

WhenAction
Day 1Confirm data looks right. Pick the first batch of invoices to chase. Send the first reminders (CFO approves).
Day 2Quick check-in message: "First reminders are out — I'll keep an eye on replies and payments."
Day 3–4Let automation run. Note any opens/replies. Flag any disputes to resolve.
Day 5Mid-pilot nudge: share an early signal — "We've sent X reminders, got Y replies so far."
Day 7One-week pulse: capture the before/after starting to form. Keep them excited.
Day 10–12Mark any payments received. Watch overdue % / DSO move. Resolve disputes.
Day 14Wrap-up call — present results, ask for the testimonial, open the paid conversation (see Sales & Conversion Playbook).

Capture the before/after

Screenshot the dashboard on Day 0 and Day 14. The visual change — overdue dropping, emails sent, payments in — is your most powerful proof and your next testimonial.

The handoff: A great onboarding flows straight into the Sales & Conversion Playbook (§6–8). Demo → onboard → first-week win → testimonial → paid. Each document picks up where the last leaves off.
