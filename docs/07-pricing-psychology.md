# Pricing Psychology & Objection Handling
### How to make $3,000/month feel like the obvious decision

---

## The Core Frame: Don't Sell the Tool, Sell the Cash

Never say "DataByt costs $3,000/month."

Always say: **"DataByt costs $3,000/month and frees up [X] in cash."**

The customer's brain anchors on the $3,000. Your job is to immediately replace that anchor with the ROI.

---

## The ROI Script (Memorize This)

**Step 1:** Ask for their revenue and current DSO.
> "What's your annual revenue roughly? And what's your current DSO — how many days does it typically take from invoice to payment?"

**Step 2:** Do the math in front of them.
```
Cash freed up = (Annual Revenue ÷ 365) × (DSO × 0.30)
```

**Step 3:** Compare to DataByt's annual cost ($36,000).
> "A 30% DSO reduction at your revenue frees up [X]. DataByt costs $36K/year. You're looking at a [multiple]× return. The cash freed up in month one typically covers the entire year's fee."

## Anchoring Strategy

**Before mentioning $3,000:**
1. Anchor on HighRadius: "HighRadius starts at $100,000/year."
2. Anchor on labor cost: "At 60% of their time, your AR team spends [X hours] on manual chasing. At [salary], that's [$Y] in wasted labor annually."
3. Anchor on bad debt: "Companies at your revenue write off $200K–$600K in bad debt annually."

**Then say:** "DataByt is $3,000/month."

Their brain now compares $36K/year to $100K (HighRadius) or $36K (labor waste) — not to $0 (doing nothing).

## The 3 Psychological Barriers (And How to Remove Them)

### Barrier 1: "Is $3,000/month worth it?"

**The reframe:**
> "$3,000/month is not a cost. It's an investment that generates [calculate ROI]× return. The question is: can you afford to leave [$X] in cash sitting in unpaid invoices?"

**Tactical:** Use the ROI calculator at databyt.in/roi in the call. Let them move the sliders. The math feels less like a sales pitch when they calculate it themselves.

---

### Barrier 2: "What if it doesn't work?"

**The reframe:**
> "Month-to-month. No annual commitment. If you don't see DSO improvement in 90 days, you cancel — no penalty, no conversation. You're risking one month of $3,000, not a year."

**Tactical:** Offer a 90-day DSO guarantee framing (not a refund, but a clear accountability metric you both agree to measure).

---

### Barrier 3: "I need to get approval from [CEO/board]."

**The reframe:**
> "Let me make that easy for you. I'll send you a one-page ROI summary with your specific numbers that you can share internally. It shows the cash freed, the cost, the comparison to HighRadius, and the 90-day DSO reduction target. Would that help?"

**Tactical:** Send the CFO a self-contained ROI memo they can forward to their CEO without any additional explanation needed.

## The Setup Fee Conversation

DataByt charges a $5,000 one-time setup fee (AR Engine).

**How to present it:**
> "There's a one-time $5,000 setup fee. That covers the integration, configuration of your email sequences, your onboarding call, and getting you live within 48 hours. After that, it's $3,000/month flat. Total Year 1 cost: $41,000."

**Compare it to:**
- "HighRadius charges $100K/year plus 12 months of implementation staff."
- "Your AR team's annual salary is probably $50K–$80K — and they're spending 60% on tasks we automate."

**If they push back on the setup fee:**
> "The setup fee covers everything needed to get you live in 48 hours — we do the work. But if it's a barrier to getting started, let me see what I can do. [Consider waiving or deferring to 30 days in]."

## The "We'll Think About It" Kill Move

When a prospect says "we'll think about it," they usually have an unspoken objection. Surface it:

> "Of course — what's the main thing you want to think through? I want to make sure you have everything you need to make the decision."

If they still won't say:
> "I've had prospects who said they'd think about it and then came back 6 months later saying they'd left $[X] on the table in uncollected AR while they waited. I'm not trying to pressure you — I just want to make sure the timing is right. What would have to be true for this to be a clear yes?"

## The Discount Policy

**Do not discount the monthly fee.** Discounting signals that the price was inflated.

**If you need to close a deal:**
- Consider waiving or reducing the setup fee
- Offer a 2-month free trial (risk is low, value is high)
- Offer a quarterly payment option instead of monthly

**Never go below $2,500/month.** It will anchor your pricing low for future customers and make it hard to raise.
