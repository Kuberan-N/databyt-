# AR Collections — Plain English Master Guide
### Know this cold before talking to any CFO

---

## 1. The Core Idea (Read This First)

**Accounts Receivable (AR) = money your business has earned but hasn't received yet.**

When a company sells to another company on credit:
- They deliver the goods or services first
- Send an invoice second
- Hope to get paid later (30, 60, or 90 days later)

Everything in between is **AR**. It's money on paper, not in your bank.

**The problem:** Large AR = cash flow problems, even if the business is profitable.

> A company can be profitable on paper and literally run out of cash — because customers are slow to pay.

---

## 2. The AR Lifecycle — 6 Steps

```
DELIVER → INVOICE → WAIT → FOLLOW UP → GET PAID → MATCH
```

| Step | What Happens | Where Problems Occur |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Deliver | Goods/services delivered | Nothing yet |
| 2. Invoice | Company sends invoice | Wrong PO number, wrong contact, missing info |
| 3. Wait | Payment terms countdown begins | Buyer forgets, delays |
| 4. Follow up | Someone chases the payment | Manual, inconsistent, scary to upset customers |
| 5. Get paid | Money arrives | Wrong amount, partial payment |
| 6. Match | Match payment to invoice in software | Manual reconciliation, errors |

## 3. The 5 Numbers CFOs Track Daily

### DSO — Days Sales Outstanding
> "On average, how many days does it take us to get paid?"

**Formula:** `DSO = (Total AR Outstanding ÷ Annual Revenue) × 365`

**Example:**
- Revenue: $10M/year
- AR outstanding: $1.5M
- DSO = (1.5M ÷ 10M) × 365 = **54.75 days**

**What's good by industry:**

| Industry | Good | Average | Bad |
|---|---|---|---|
| SaaS / Software | < 30 days | 35–45 days | > 60 days |
| Professional Services | < 35 days | 42 days | > 60 days |
| Manufacturing | < 45 days | 50–60 days | > 75 days |
| Construction | < 60 days | 65–90 days | > 90 days |

**Why it matters to you as a founder:**
At $10M revenue, every 1 day of DSO = **$27,397 tied up in AR**.
Reduce DSO by 15 days → **$411,000 freed** in cash.
This is how you anchor every sales conversation.

### AR Aging — Who Owes What, For How Long

A snapshot of all unpaid invoices grouped by overdue time:

| Bucket | Risk Level | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Current (not yet due) | No risk | None |
| 1–30 days overdue | Low | Polite reminder |
| 31–60 days overdue | Medium | Firm follow-up |
| 61–90 days overdue | High | Escalation |
| 90+ days overdue | Critical | Human intervention / legal |

**Key fact:** Invoice collection rates drop sharply with age.
- 90+ days: < 70% collected
- 6 months: ~50% collected
- 12 months: < 25% collected

The longer you wait, the more you lose.

---

### CEI — Collections Effectiveness Index
> "Out of everything we could have collected, how much did we actually collect?"

**Formula:**
`CEI = (Amount Collected ÷ Amount Collectible) × 100`

| Score | Status |
|---|---|
| 90–100 | Excellent |
| 80–89 | Good |
| 70–79 | Needs improvement |
| < 70 | Serious problem |

DataByt shows this as a live gauge in the dashboard. It's the CFO's report card.

### Bad Debt Rate
> "What % of our revenue do we just write off and never collect?"

**Industry average:** 1–3% of annual revenue.

On a $20M business → that's $200K–$600K per year, gone.

DataByt impact: systematic early follow-up catches invoices before they become bad debt.

---

### DPO — Days Payable Outstanding
> "How long does it take us to pay OUR suppliers?"

High DPO = good for the buyer (they keep cash longer).
High DSO = bad for the seller (they wait for cash).

CFOs are always trying to: **maximize DPO + minimize DSO** at the same time.

## 4. Dunning — What It Is

**Dunning** = the formal process of systematically following up with customers to collect payment.

It's not "being aggressive." It's structured, scheduled, professional communication.

**The 3 Levels:**

| Level | Trigger | Tone | Subject Line Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Just overdue | Friendly, assumes oversight | "Friendly reminder — Invoice #1234 due last week" |
| L2 | 15–30 days overdue | Direct, references L1 | "Invoice #1234 is 20 days overdue — action needed" |
| L3 | 30–60 days overdue | Serious, final warning | "FINAL NOTICE — Invoice #1234 — Account at risk" |
| L4+ | 60+ days | Human takes over | Phone call, legal letter, collections agency |

DataByt automates L1, L2, L3 with AI. Each email contains the customer's real name, real invoice number, real amount, and a direct payment link.

## 5. Manual AR vs. Automated AR

**What a typical finance team does today:**

1. Pulls aging report from QuickBooks every Monday (30 min)
2. Decides who to email based on memory or largest balances
3. Manually writes or copy-pastes the dunning email for each customer
4. Logs it in a spreadsheet (sometimes)
5. Forgets to follow up because they're busy
6. Dispute comes in → sits in email for weeks → nobody tracks it
7. CFO asks "why is our DSO 65 days?" → nobody knows

**The result:** Inconsistent. Some customers get chased hard. Some don't get chased at all. Invoices age into bad debt.
