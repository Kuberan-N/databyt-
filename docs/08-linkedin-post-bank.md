### Post 8 — The CEI Explainer Post
```
Most AR teams track DSO. Almost none track CEI.

CEI stands for Collections Effectiveness Index.

DSO tells you how long it takes to get paid.
CEI tells you how much of what you could collect, you actually collected.

The formula:
CEI = (Amount Collected ÷ Amount Collectible) × 100

A CEI of 80 = you collected 80% of everything you could have collected.
A CEI of 100 = perfect (you collected everything collectible).
A CEI of 60 = you're losing 40% of collectible revenue somehow.

Why it matters more than DSO:
DSO can look good if you write off a lot of bad debt (the uncollected invoices leave the AR).
CEI shows you the real picture — what you should have collected vs. what you did.

DataByt shows CEI as a live gauge in the dashboard.

It's the one number that tells a CFO how healthy their collections operation actually is.
```

### Post 19 — The "Dunning" Word Explanation
```
"Dunning" is one of those finance words that sounds harsh.

It's not.

Dunning is just: systematically communicating with customers who haven't paid, in a way that maintains the relationship while moving toward payment.

Done well, it sounds like this:

"Hi Sarah, just a quick note — Invoice #1234 for $5,000 is now 15 days past due. I've included the payment link below so you can sort it quickly. Let me know if you have any questions."

That's not aggressive. That's helpful. You're doing the customer a favor by making it easy.

The problem with manual dunning is inconsistency:
→ Some customers get chased. Some don't.
→ Some get emails. Some get calls.
→ Some disputes get resolved. Some sit for months.

Automated dunning is just: consistent, professional follow-up for every invoice, every time.

Nothing aggressive about it.
```
